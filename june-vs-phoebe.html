Where June takes a different line.

One job, done properly

June is built around one outcome: the open shift gets covered. No suite of assistants to configure, just coverage that runs on its own, day and night.

A person is always reachable

When a shift is unusual, sensitive or not going well, June hands it to a live scheduler with the entire conversation attached, not a summary that loses the details.

Outreach that matches the urgency

A shift twelve hours out and a shift starting in forty minutes are not treated the same. Standard, urgent and critical each get their own pace and channel.

Three questions to ask either of us.

What happens when the AI can't fill a shift on its own?

Fully autonomous coverage is excellent on the routine eighty percent. The other twenty, a difficult client, a caregiver with a complaint, a shift nobody wants, is where agencies get hurt. June's handoff is deliberate and fast, and the scheduler who picks it up reads exactly what the caregiver said, word for word.

In what order are my caregivers contacted?

Contacting everyone at once fills a shift quickly and teaches your bench that most messages aren't for them. June orders outreach so the caregivers most likely to say yes hear first, and everyone else hears from you only when it's relevant.

How long does it take to get up and running?

A suite of assistants means a longer rollout, more setup and more places for the process to drift. June does one thing, so you can judge it on one number: how many shifts got covered, and how fast.
