June asks the right caregivers first.

  1. Caregivers who know the clientThey've worked with this client before and they show up.
  2. Reliable caregivers new to the clientNext, if the first group can't take it.
  3. Everyone else who qualifiesWeighed by hours already worked that week and the drive.

Anyone who fails a certification, language, client preference or hours rule is never asked.

Your scheduler steps in when it matters.

June hands the shift to a person, with every word so far attached, when:

  • Every eligible caregiver was asked and nobody said yes
  • The shift is about to start and is still open
  • A caregiver would go past an hours limit
  • A caregiver asks for a person

Why ClearDesk built June.

We run scheduling for home care agencies. The hardest part of that job is the callout: a caregiver can't make it, and someone has to find the next person who can go.

Most of that work is the same every time. Check who fits, text, wait, call, ask again. June does that part, day and night, so your schedulers keep the judgment calls.
